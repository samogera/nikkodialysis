01 · Haemodialysis

Cleaning your blood when your kidneys can't.

A machine filters your blood through a dialyser, an artificial kidney, removing waste and extra fluid, then returns it to you. Most people come three times a week for about four hours.

A session, step by step
First 15 minutesCheck-in and checks

We weigh you, take your blood pressure and temperature, and ask how you have been since your last session.

About 15 minutesConnecting to the machine

Your nurse cleans and checks your access, then connects you while you settle into a reclining chair.

About 4 hoursYour blood is cleaned

The machine filters your blood while nurses monitor you and the machine throughout. Read, rest, watch TV or work.

Last 20 minutesDisconnect and recover

We disconnect you, check your access site and weigh you again, then confirm your next session.

02 · Holiday & visiting dialysis

Travel to Nairobi. Don't miss a session.

A family wedding, a business trip or a safari: we reserve your chairs and work directly with your home unit.

Request holiday sessions

Send us at least 2 weeks before travel

  • Referral letter and dialysis prescription from your nephrologist
  • Recent blood results, including hepatitis B, C and HIV screening
  • Your last three treatment records and current medicines
  • Travel dates and how you will pay: insurance or self-pay

03 · Access care

Your fistula is your lifeline.

We check your fistula, graft or catheter every session, teach you how to protect it, and refer you quickly to a vascular surgeon if anything changes.

Fistula checksCatheter careSurgical referral

04 · Diet & education

Eat well, the Kenyan way.

Fluid limits, salt, potassium and phosphate, explained with everyday meals like ugali, sukuma and githeri. Take-home guides for whoever cooks at home.

05 · Labs & reviews

A plan that keeps up with you.

Routine blood tests and regular nephrologist reviews keep your prescription right. When you are ready, we coordinate transplant work-up referrals.
